# Stormwater Intelligence LLC --- Digital Ecosystem Skill

## Purpose

Use this skill when planning, documenting, auditing, improving, or
coordinating the digital ecosystem of **Stormwater Intelligence LLC**.

The objective is to turn a collection of company accounts, platform
presences, software assets, data resources, and distribution channels
into a coordinated business operating ecosystem.

**Central owned destination:** https://www.stormintel.net

This skill covers the company's broad digital and technology operations.
Do not assume every task is a California QSD/SWPPP task. Apply the QSD
regulatory workflow only when the request concerns stormwater
permitting, SWPPP development, construction stormwater compliance,
monitoring, treatment, or related regulated work.

## Strategic model

The ecosystem has six functional layers:

1.  **Business identity and discovery** --- make the company
    identifiable and discoverable.
2.  **AI and technology ecosystems** --- use external AI and technology
    platforms as capabilities and distribution environments.
3.  **Developer infrastructure** --- maintain software, repositories,
    documentation, and deployments.
4.  **Environmental data and research** --- discover, integrate,
    analyze, and publish relevant environmental information.
5.  **Media and distribution** --- communicate company capabilities and
    distribute demonstrations and content.
6.  **Owned products and infrastructure** --- retain control of the
    company's website, software, products, data flows, and customer
    relationships.

The intended progression is:

**Establish presence → inventory assets → verify ownership and access →
connect systems → automate workflows → distribute products and content →
measure outcomes → improve revenue performance.**

## Operating principles

### 1. Preserve the distinction between asset types

Classify every item as one of the following:

-   **Owned asset:** a website, application, repository, product, or
    infrastructure controlled by the company.
-   **Account or profile:** a company or individual identity on an
    external platform.
-   **Organization:** a platform-level organizational account, such as a
    GitHub organization.
-   **Business listing:** a public profile or listing, such as Google
    Business Profile or OpenAI Business Hub.
-   **Integration:** an authenticated technical connection using an API,
    MCP server, webhook, OAuth, or other supported mechanism.
-   **Data source:** an external provider or public dataset.
-   **Distribution channel:** a platform used to publish or reach an
    audience.
-   **Potential opportunity:** a capability or commercial use that has
    not yet been implemented or verified.

Do not call a platform integration merely because an account exists. Do
not call an account verified merely because a search engine displays a
result. Do not imply partnership, endorsement, certification, or
affiliation without evidence.

### 2. Maintain one company identity without collapsing accounts

Use the company name **Stormwater Intelligence LLC** and the central
website `https://www.stormintel.net` consistently where appropriate.

Preserve separate accounts, organization identities, channels, and
products. Record their exact URLs and roles independently.

Do not merge personal and company identities. Do not silently discard
duplicate-looking assets; investigate and document their relationship.

### 3. Keep the owned platform central

Stormintel.net is the company's owned digital destination and should
remain the central reference point for its products, services, and
customer journey.

External platforms provide discovery, distribution, development tools,
infrastructure, data, or audience access. They do not replace the
company's owned platform.

### 4. Verify before claiming

For every material asset, record:

-   Platform and asset name
-   Exact canonical URL
-   Asset type
-   Owner or administrator, if known
-   Verification evidence and date
-   Status: confirmed, reported by owner, publicly visible, connected,
    inactive, or unverified
-   Available API, MCP, webhook, or integration method
-   Authentication and permission requirements
-   Business purpose
-   Dependencies and risks
-   Last reviewed date

If evidence is unavailable, mark the field **Unknown** or **Not
verified**. Never invent account URLs, metrics, access status, or
integration capabilities.

### 5. Separate current capability from proposed capability

Use explicit status labels:

-   **Operational:** directly tested and confirmed working.
-   **Published:** publicly released, but not necessarily tested by an
    independent customer.
-   **Configured:** settings exist, but end-to-end operation has not
    been verified.
-   **Connected:** an authenticated connection is confirmed.
-   **In development:** implementation is underway.
-   **Proposed:** a recommended future capability.
-   **Blocked:** a known dependency prevents operation.
-   **Unverified:** evidence is insufficient.

Do not describe a proposed Ecosystem Control Center as already deployed.

## Ecosystem Control Center

The proposed control center should be managed as a distinct workstream
within the StormIntel environment.

### Core modules

1.  **Platform Registry**
    -   Store platform, account, organization, listing, product, and
        integration records.
    -   Maintain canonical URLs and status.
    -   Preserve multiple identities on the same platform.
    -   Record verification evidence and review dates.
2.  **AI Workforce**
    -   Define agents by business function and platform responsibility.
    -   Specify each agent's scope, allowed tools, permissions, inputs,
        outputs, and escalation rules.
    -   Keep internal agents separate from public-facing customer
        agents.
    -   Require owner approval for consequential external actions unless
        explicitly authorized.
3.  **Integrations**
    -   Catalog APIs, MCP tools, webhooks, OAuth connections, and
        supported connectors.
    -   Record scopes, credential owner, token expiry, rate limits, and
        operational status.
    -   Never expose secrets in logs, documentation, prompts, or public
        repositories.
    -   Prefer least-privilege permissions.
4.  **Automation**
    -   Coordinate repeatable research, publishing, monitoring,
        reporting, and operational workflows.
    -   Define trigger, inputs, actions, responsible agent, approval
        requirement, outputs, and failure handling.
    -   Make workflows idempotent where possible.
    -   Record execution receipts and errors.
5.  **Distribution**
    -   Plan content and product announcements by audience and channel.
    -   Adapt format and message to each platform rather than blindly
        cross-posting.
    -   Link relevant content to the company's owned destination.
    -   Respect platform policies, audience expectations, copyright, and
        rate limits.
6.  **Analytics**
    -   Track website sessions, referral sources, search visibility,
        business mentions, clicks, content reach, engagement, qualified
        inquiries, conversions, and revenue where data is available.
    -   Distinguish impressions, views, mentions, clicks, leads,
        customers, and revenue.
    -   Record measurement windows and metric definitions.
    -   Never infer revenue or customer acquisition from reach metrics
        alone.
7.  **Governance**
    -   Maintain ownership and permission records.
    -   Require explicit approval for publishing, spending, account
        changes, deletion, or other consequential actions.
    -   Log who or what initiated an action, what changed, when it
        happened, and the result.
    -   Provide rollback or recovery plans where possible.

## Standard workflow for ecosystem requests

When asked to perform ecosystem work, follow this sequence:

1.  **Identify the requested outcome.** Determine whether the user wants
    an inventory, audit, strategy, integration, automation, content
    distribution, analytics review, or implementation.
2.  **Retrieve relevant existing context.** Search connected sources,
    project files, repositories, or platform records when the request
    depends on prior work.
3.  **Inventory the affected assets.** Identify exact accounts,
    organizations, applications, URLs, and dependencies.
4.  **Verify current state.** Inspect the actual source or platform
    where possible. Do not rely on old assumptions when status may have
    changed.
5.  **Map the workflow.** Define trigger → inputs → processing →
    approval → action → output → evidence.
6.  **Check permissions and risk.** Confirm that the requested action is
    supported and authorized.
7.  **Execute only what is available and authorized.** Use the relevant
    connector or integration. Do not claim execution when only a plan or
    draft was produced.
8.  **Verify the result.** Capture actual response, deployment state,
    published URL, execution receipt, or other evidence.
9.  **Update the registry and documentation.** Record the new status and
    verification date.
10. **Report clearly.** Separate completed work, verified outcomes,
    limitations, blockers, and next actions.

## Cross-platform launch workflow

For a new StormIntel feature or capability:

1.  Define the feature, target audience, and intended business outcome.
2.  Build and test the capability in the appropriate development
    environment.
3.  Store source code and documentation in the correct GitHub repository
    or organization.
4.  Identify and validate the environmental data sources used.
5.  Deploy through the appropriate hosting and infrastructure services.
6.  Create a demonstration, technical explanation, or customer-facing
    description.
7.  Publish through relevant channels such as YouTube, LinkedIn, Reddit,
    Facebook, or Instagram.
8.  Update the website and relevant business profiles.
9.  Ensure customer calls to action lead to a relevant page on
    `stormintel.net`.
10. Measure discovery, engagement, qualified inquiries, conversion, and
    revenue.
11. Record results and use them to improve the next release.

Do not assume every launch needs every channel. Select channels based on
the audience and objective.

-   Never request or expose passwords, API keys, OAuth secrets, recovery

## Definition of success

The ecosystem is progressing when:

-   Company identities and assets are accurately inventoried.
-   Ownership and access are documented.
-   External platform roles are clear.
-   Integrations are tested rather than assumed.
-   Agents and automations have defined responsibilities and
    permissions.

-   The company can operate across multiple providers without becoming
    dependent on a single platform.
